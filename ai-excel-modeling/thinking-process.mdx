Apers shows you exactly what it plans to do to your spreadsheet. By default it then makes the change right away. If you'd rather approve each change first, turn off auto-approve.

## How Apers Thinks

When you give Apers a task, it first creates a plan. You can see:

- **Thinking process**: How Apers is strategizing the model
- **Planned changes**: Which cells it will update, create, or change
- **Content preview**: The numbers and formulas Apers will use

This transparency lets you follow everything Apers does.

## Auto-Approve

Auto-approve is on by default: Apers makes its changes without pausing for your approval. It applies in every chat you start, and a workflow follows the same setting. A chat started by a [stage goal](/deals/pipeline-automation#stage-goals) uses that goal's own setting.

To approve each change yourself, turn off the **Auto-approve** toggle in the chat composer. Apers then pauses before every change and waits for you. Your choice is remembered in that browser. Your phone has its own setting (see [Apers on Your Phone](/mobile)).

<Warning>
  With auto-approve on, Apers keeps working without stopping for your review, which can use more credits than expected.
</Warning>

## Approval Options

With auto-approve off, Apers pauses after it shows its plan, and you choose what to do:

<ResponseField name="Approve" type="action">
  Approves this single update. Apers will make the change and then pause for your next approval.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Always Approve" type="action">
  Approves this change and turns **auto-approve** back on. Apers will then make changes without pausing for approval, in every chat, until you turn it off again.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Reject" type="action">
  Stops Apers from making the change. You can type a reason explaining why, and Apers will adjust its approach.
</ResponseField>

## When to Reject

Sometimes Apers might:

- Misunderstand your instructions
- Use different assumptions than you expected
- Change something you wanted to keep

Just click **Reject**, type your feedback, and Apers will try again with your guidance.

<Info>
  Rejecting with a clear reason helps Apers learn what you want for the rest of the task.
</Info>
